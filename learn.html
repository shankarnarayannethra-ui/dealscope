M&A, explained clearly

A concise guide to the terms, mechanics, and market reactions that shape merger and acquisition analysis.

Why do target shares often rise while acquirer shares may dip?

Target shareholders are typically offered a premium above the pre-announcement market price. Acquirer investors may react cautiously because the buyer is paying that premium, taking on debt, issuing shares, or assuming integration risk. These are tendencies, not rules.

Acquisition

One company purchases control of another company or its assets.

Merger

Two companies combine into a single legal or economic organization.

Enterprise value

A measure that incorporates equity value, debt, and cash to estimate the value of the whole business.

Synergies

Expected benefits from combining operations, such as lower costs or additional revenue.

Accretion / dilution

Whether the deal is expected to increase or decrease earnings per share.

Goodwill

The accounting premium paid above the fair value of identifiable net assets.

Breakup fee

A payment that may be owed when a signed transaction fails under specified conditions.

Tender offer

A direct offer to shareholders to sell their shares at stated terms.

Hostile takeover

An acquisition attempt pursued without the target board’s support.
